What does a space pony actually look like?

The vertical slice runs on gray-box capsules — physics is real, art isn't, on purpose. Genetics can warp a pony's leg count, tail type and silhouette across generations, so "pony" is a vibe, not a body plan. Below are three honest, fully-realized directions for what that vibe could look like on screen. Pick one, or steal pieces from all three.

the reskin nobody's picked yet

Three directions, three different bets

Each one commits to a different silhouette, a different production cost, and a different reason it should feel wholesome instead of weird-for-weird's-sake. None of them is the "safe" choice — that's rather the point of exploring this in parallel with engineering.

Direction one

Faceted Frontier

featured mutation: 3 rocket-boot legs, antigrav horn

Flat-shaded, low-vertex geometry in the spirit of PS1-era 3D and vertex-lit Katamari-adjacent worlds — chunky and jagged enough that it reads as goofy on sight, never sinister. Low-poly silhouettes already look like they're vibrating at low frame rates, so the sine-wave wobble the controller adds basically sells itself instead of fighting the model. It's also the cheapest of the three for a small Godot team: simple meshes, a single flat/toon shader, and new breeding body-plans (swap a leg count, re-facet a horn) are fast blockouts rather than full re-sculpts.

Godot build effort low
Direction two

The Doughnaut

featured mutation: 4 noodle-tentacle legs, third eye

Hand-pinched clay with visible thumbprints, lopsided proportions and soft matte shading, in the spirit of Aardman and Laika stop-motion. This is the direction where "janky by design" stops needing an excuse — a wobbly, imperfect surface is exactly what real clay looks like, so the procedural bob reads as material, not as a bug. It's also the hardest sell for a small team: faking clay convincingly in real-time (matte sub-surface look, dimple texture, asymmetric silhouettes) needs real shader work, and every new bred body-plan is closer to a fresh sculpt than a simple mesh swap.

Godot build effort high
Direction three

Cutout Cosmos

featured mutation: paper wings, stacked flame rocket-tail

Bold flat color, thick ink outlines and offset "paper layer" drop-shadows, closer to collage/cutout animation than 3D at all. Candy-bright and blunt-shaped, it lands wholesome-absurd through sheer cheerfulness and reads instantly at a glance during a frantic four-way race — exactly when a busier style would turn to noise. It's the cheapest and fastest of the three to produce and reskin per breeding trait (flat meshes or billboards, no complex shading), but flat shapes can look pasted-on in a physics-driven 3D track unless the team commits to billboarding or careful faceting to keep it feeling embedded in the world rather than stickered onto it.

Godot build effort low–medium

Three ways to build Faceted Frontier

"Low-poly" isn't one look — it's a whole family, from PS1 mascot games to Minecraft to a scratched 1998 demo disc. These three riffs commit fully to the same rig as Direction One (angular facets, the three mismatched rocket-boot legs, the glowing antigrav horn) and just change what happens at render time — a closer look at how far the cheapest direction on this page can be pushed before a call has to be made.

Faceted Frontier · Riff one

Vaportrail

3 rocket-boot legs, antigrav horn — chrome + neon finish

Same low-vertex rig as the base direction, but the shading leans into early-3D nostalgia instead of hiding it: glossy chrome-gradient facets, a banded vaporwave sun, and a perspective grid horizon standing in for a whole environment. The antigrav horn becomes a literal neon beacon — a pony that's part racehorse, part screensaver. The retro-tech glow reads as playful kitsch, not edgy, keeping it in Goat-Simulator territory rather than tryhard sci-fi. Build cost stays close to the base direction — same meshes, same rig — plus one gradient/fresnel facet shader and a simple grid-and-sun backdrop, so it's still a cheap reskin, just not the cheapest.

Godot build effort medium
Faceted Frontier · Riff two

Blockfoal

3 rocket-boot legs, antigrav horn — built from unit cubes

Swap flat-shaded polygons for visible unit cubes and the whole pony reads as built rather than sculpted — three blocky rocket-boot legs, a stacked-cube horn, a mane that steps like pixel stairs. It leans on instantly-familiar block-game vernacular, which does a lot of the "why is this charming" work for free, and a construction-toy pony is funny by default, so wholesome-absurd barely needs selling. It's also the cheapest thing on this whole page to build and reskin: every breeding mutation is just adding, removing, or recoloring cubes on a shared unit-cube kit — no new topology, no custom shader beyond flat/toon shading already in use elsewhere.

Godot build effort low
Faceted Frontier · Riff three

Disc Rot

3 rocket-boot legs, antigrav horn — dithered demo-disc finish

Same faceted rig again, but the render pipeline itself is the bit: ordered-dither checkerboard shading instead of smooth gradients, jittery vertex-snap wobble, faint chromatic-aberration fringing, and scanlines — the exact artifacts of a warped 1998 demo disc. It pushes "janky by design" a step further than the base direction, because the instability other styles fight to hide becomes the whole joke here, and it pairs naturally with retro framing (loading screens, fake memory-card icons) that keeps wholesome-absurd feeling like a bit, not a budget compromise. Geometry stays as cheap as the base direction, but the full effect needs real shader work — a dither post-process, a vertex-snap shader, a chromatic-aberration pass — tuned so it doesn't fight Godot's own anti-aliasing. The priciest of the three riffs, though still lighter than a full claymation build.

Godot build effort high

Faceted Frontier, rigged and running

A mockup can't show what the procedural wobble in pony_controller.gd actually feels like at runtime, so here's a live WebGL build of Direction One instead of another still frame — flat-shaded primitives standing in for real low-poly meshes, animated the same way the game does it: no hand-keyframes, just sine waves layered onto a bone hierarchy, scaled by speed. Same rig, running live

root → body → {head, tail, leg × 3} — procedural, not skeletal

Built as a hierarchy of independent parts, exactly like the game's own approach: real transforms driven by sine waves and layered onto a base pose, not a single hand-authored animation clip — which is also why it doesn't care that the three rocket-boot legs are mismatched lengths. It's still the cheapest direction on this page to build; this is just that same cheapness proven out in motion.

None of these are final.

Mixing is fair game — a Faceted-Frontier body with Doughnaut-style asymmetric proportions, or Cutout Cosmos color logic over a low-poly mesh, are all reasonable next sketches. The goal here is a direction to reskin M1's gray-box capsule toward at M5, not a locked art bible.
